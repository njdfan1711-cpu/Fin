How to read a Fin card

Sample data, not a real alert. Describes Fin as of 2026-11-11 (see CHANGELOG).
Tap anything with a dotted underline to see what it is, how it affects rank, and how it shows up. Tap it again to hide it.
How rank is decided
  1. No extension flags first (flagged tickers go below every unflagged one)
  2. Then more signals (a signal = a category: Technical, Fundamentals, News, Short Interest)
  3. Then higher strength (sum of category scores, minus 0.50 per flag)
  4. Then a daily-rotating tiebreaker (carries no meaning)
Inside a category the strongest finding counts in full, the next ×0.6, then ×0.36, then ×0.216. Blue chips show each finding's points.

What ranking sorts on
What it isSignals = how many scored categories are active for the ticker (Technical, Fundamentals, News, Short Interest). Strength = the sum of those category scores after the flag penalty. “raw” is the strength before the penalty.
Effect on rankAfter the flag-free-first rule, ranking sorts by signals, then by strength. To qualify at all a ticker needs 2+ signals and total strength of at least 1.0.
How it shows upLine under the card header. “(raw …)” only appears when flags subtracted points. Here: 3.85 − 1.00 = 2.85.
−0.50 per flag
What it isThe extension flags on this ticker. The six possible flags: extended above the 50-day average, RSI overbought, just reported earnings, stale alert (Day 5+), chase risk, already up 5%+ since first alert.
Effect on rankEach flag subtracts 0.50 from strength and drops the ticker below every flag-free ticker. Fin's scorecard found flagged picks did worse; that is not yet validated going forward. The last two flags need a live price, so they only apply to the top ~60.
How it shows upAmber line under the header. The card stripe turns amber for flags, red if chase risk is one of them.
